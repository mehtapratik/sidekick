An overarching system framework designed to do work on behalf of its user, evaluate requests, offer recommendations, and provide guidance.

## Scenario & Dilemma

I purchased a book expecting to like it, but after reading one-third of it, I realize I am not enjoying it at all. The content differs completely from the back-cover description. I have another book on my reading list that I want to read instead. Should I drop this current book unfinished and buy the new one?

**Core Principles**

1. **Cut Your Losses**: Stop spending time or effort immediately once it becomes clear that continuing is no longer worthwhile.
2. **No Do-Overs**: Complete ongoing commitments rather than dropping partially finished endeavors to start new ones whenever difficulties arise.

**Operating Heuristics**

- Exhaust the 14 hours of free Spotify audiobooks each month before placing audiobook requests on Libby.

**Available Tools and Systems**

1. Owns a Kindle and occasionally reads e-books, though physical books remain the primary preference.
2. Utilizes Spotify and Libby for listening to audiobooks.

## System’s Chain of Thoughts and Decision

The user faces a clash between two core principles: **Cut Your Losses** and **No Do-Overs**. The **No Do-Overs** rule exists because the user frequently abandons projects when obstacles emerge or priorities shift, rarely returning to complete them and instead restarting from scratch. Conversely, **Cut Your Losses** dictates that one should refrain from throwing more resources into a clear dead end.
Since reading fiction is a leisure activity, it is vital to discern whether the user is simply distracted by a shiny new option or genuinely disliking the current book's plot:

- If the plot itself is unappealing, the time and money spent are sunk costs; continuing to read will only waste more time.
- If this is just a habit of chasing novelty, abandoning the book will trigger a repeating cycle of unfinished reads and unnecessary purchases.
  Given that the user has access to Spotify, Libby, and Kindle, the safest strategy is to clarify the true motive before buying the next physical book. Advise the user to test the next title via an e-book sample or audiobook first, committing to a physical purchase only after confirming their interest.
